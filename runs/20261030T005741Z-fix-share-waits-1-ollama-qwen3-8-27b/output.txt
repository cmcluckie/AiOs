> /* Wait for one byte, then take whatever else has already arrived.  The wait
>  * itself keeps interrupts on: a tick may arrive in the middle of it and hand
>  * the processor to the next program, which resumes the waiter where it was
>  * (its registers are saved on its kernel stack by the gate) when its own turn
>  * comes again.  Only the taking of the byte closes the interrupts, so a tick
>  * never lands in the middle of it and one program's bytes never interleave
>  * with another's. */
