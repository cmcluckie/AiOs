AiOS, part 6.  "The test went red, and it was right: 99999999999 is not 1215752191."...

The machine listens now.  Finally, something in my house that does.

Part 6 is a reverse-Polish calculator, the one from the stack chapter.  Type "3 4 +", it says "= 7".  "2 *", "= 14".  If you ever owned an HP calculator you're nodding right now.  It's the first program on AiOS that sits there and waits for a person.

Reading input has history for me.  In C I grew to really appreciate stdio.h: printf, scanf, getchar, simple and honest.  Then I graduated to C++ and met cin >> x, and those two greater-than signs made no sense to me.  Still don't, honestly, to this day.  So it's a little funny that on AiOS there's neither.  No stdio, no >>.  The program asks the machine for one byte at a time and builds everything else itself.

To make that happen, three little things grew.  The serial driver we took from Linux got its other half, the handful of lines that ask the chip "got a byte for me?" and take it.  The loader got a fourth door, read, next to write, exit and alloc.  Thirteen lines.  And the calculator had to learn everything your terminal normally does behind your back: show you what you typed, handle backspace, figure out that Enter is a different key depending on whether a person or a script is typing.  Nothing here does anything for you.  It's kind of refreshing.

Here's the part I love.  Before I booted it, I had the same calculator written a second time in Python, from the same rules, as a reference.  The test types the same keys into both and compares every byte.

It went red.  I typed 99999999999 to check the "too big" error and it cheerfully told me "= 1215752191".  On a 32-bit machine the overflow check was running after the number had already wrapped around.  One line to fix.  That's the first time on this ladder a test caught something we'd gotten wrong, and it's exactly why rule 8 says every test has to be able to fail.

Also: divide by zero.  On your laptop the operating system catches that and kills the program.  This one has nobody to catch it; the whole machine would just quietly reset.  So the calculator says no before it tries.  Teaching the landlord to evict a tenant who breaks stuff will be its own rung someday.

Ladder held again.  Hello world, Hanoi and Life all run on the new loader, untouched.

Hit F5, pick calc, and go nuts.  Well.  Within 32 bits.
