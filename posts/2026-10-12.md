AiOS, part 2.  "An operating system where nothing exists unless someone asked for it."...

Part 1 was the result.  This is the rulebook that produced it, in plain English.  Ten rules, and no vote can change them.

1. No purpose, no operating system.  Everything in the machine traces to a written reason for being there.  Not "it came with the distribution".  A reason.

2. One door.  The only way anything gets in, a driver, a program, a test, the loader itself, is a request that names what it needs and why.  No side entrances.

3. Source only.  Nothing pre-compiled.  Everything is built from source you can read, all the way down to the compiler.

4. Reviewed before entry.  Against this machine's purpose, every time.  Review is a record of who looked, never a claim of "proven safe".

5. No waiting on anyone upstream.  Once the source is in, it's ours.  Upstream becomes a supplier of suggestions, not an authority we wait on for a fix.

6. Mechanism and policy stay apart.  The core provides the levers.  It never decides when to pull them.

7. Intelligence and authority never share a body.  The AI proposes.  A dumb, deliberately unclever referee decides, by checking that every part traces to a live request.  There is nothing to sweet-talk.

8. A test is written to fail.  A test that can't go red is decoration.  Before anything is trusted, somebody breaks it on purpose and watches the test notice.

9. Nothing is self-reported.  No program's confidence counts, no AI's confidence counts.  Confidence is earned by track record.

10. Everything expires.  A permission nobody uses gets revoked.  A component nobody asks for gets stripped.

Number ten is the one Linux can't do.  It can add, forever.  It can never take back out.  Same parts, opposite selection pressure.  That's the whole difference.
