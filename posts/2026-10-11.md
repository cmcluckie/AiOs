AiOS, part 1.  "Linux is tens of millions of lines of code.  I took 57 of them"...

I love AI and I love learning, so I'm building an operating system on the side, from nothing up, and sharing it here as it goes.  A chance for all of us to learn how one actually works.  Expect wrong turns.  Pushback welcome.

Last night it booted for the first time.

Linux is tens of millions of lines of code.  I took 57 of them: the lines that put a byte on a serial port, lifted out of the serial driver with everything they were holding onto cut away.  I wrote 113 more: a tiny loader that takes over from the machine, hands the program two things it's allowed to do, write and stop, and pulls the plug when it's done.  And a hello world of 7 lines that only ever talks through those two doors.

170 lines.  It boots in a virtual machine, fourteen bytes come out the serial port, nothing before, nothing after, and the machine shuts itself off.  Most of those 170 lines can tell you who asked for them and why.  I have a list of the ones that can't.

It is not a smaller Linux.  It's Linux re-admitted one justified line at a time, and the rules for what gets admitted are the actual project.  That's part 2.

An AI is doing the digging through kernel source so I don't have to.  I'm making the calls.  The repo is public and every commit is signed.

I don't know where this ends up.  That's the point.  I'm a dad, so posts come as often as life allows.

Come along!
