AiOS, part 7.  "I've spent years asking teams for specs and tests.  Six posts in, I realised I hadn't done it myself."...

This one's a confession, and probably the most useful post in the series so far.

My day job is product management, on software that's been around a long time.  The thing I care about most with an old product is being able to answer the basic questions about any piece of it.  What was asked for?  What actually got built?  How do we know it works?  When nobody can answer those, the product gets scary to touch.

So, six posts into my own project, I went back and asked those questions about everything we'd built.  And I couldn't answer them.  Not properly.  A couple of parts had no written description of what they actually do.  One had no spec at all.  And the tests: we had sixteen of them, written down, each saying exactly what would make it fail.  Almost none had ever been run by anything except me, by hand, eyeballing the output.

I'd done the exact thing I nag other people about.  I was building fast, it was fun, and the boring stuff slid.

So we stopped and fixed it.  Every part now has its "as built": a plain description of what it really does, every port it touches, every byte it writes.  Every spec has a test.  Every blog post now ends with an addendum listing every test for the parts in it, and honestly whether it has been run.  A lot of them say "not run yet."  That's the truth, so that's what they say.

And then the bit that stops it happening again.  There's now a section in the project rules literally called "Definition of done": nine things that have to be true before anything counts as finished, or gets a post.  And two little scripts that check it and name what's missing.  I ran the checker against this morning's version of the repo and it failed, on exactly the two gaps I'd found by hand.  Against today's, it passes.

None of this is clever.  It's the stuff every team knows it should do and skips when it's having fun.  Writing it down as a rule, and having a dumb script that won't let it slide, is the only thing I've ever seen actually work.

Next up: the thing that actually runs all those tests, so "checked by hand" can finally become "checked."
