# Findings 0033: the timer, seeing the compiler, and what the test said next

Source: part 23, after findings 0032 and decision 0111.  The rerun of the timer
on the layered bench, 2026-11-07, at 883ea0f, the plan agent/plans/race-timer.toml
unchanged; the two cut-short reruns of 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-01, and the old
bench's last timer file, read by Claude Code in WSL on 2026-11-01.  Every number
is from the run records named.  Goes to: the architects (Chris and Claude).

1. **The reruns of 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-01 do not count.**  On the first,
   20261031T205725Z-timer-1, the card failed at call 43 ("CUDA error: unknown
   error"); every call after it took about 20 minutes, and the three runs that
   followed got 12, 24 and 18 calls in four hours each (out/race.log).  Before
   the failure the fix worked: hand-ins 1 and 2 were shown the compiler's and the
   linker's words, and hand-in 3 built.

2. **Run 1, 2026-11-07: red, two builders of 60 calls each, then the director
   stopped.**  runs/20261107T190845Z-timer-1 and 20261107T201247Z-timer-2: 22
   hand-ins judged, 6 that did not build and 16 that built and failed with "the
   door said 0 throughout".  The time on each line says which: 11 of the 16 took
   1 second, 5 took 11.  The director, runs/20261107T201305Z, then asked
   strategic what the item's file must define and what its two tests check.  Run 2
   was stopped by Chris before any hand-in; runs 2 and 3 were not run.

3. **Built and still 0: two causes, read by Claude Code in WSL under emulation,
   not on the jig.**
   - 20261031T205725Z-timer-1's divide names the wrong operand: `divl %3` divides
     by the high word of the number being divided, not by the divisor, which is
     %4 (its tsccal.c line 86).  The quotient overflows, the processor raises a
     divide error before the first line is printed, and the machine resets.  With
     %3 made %4 the test passes.
   - 20261031T024306Z-timer-2 waits on the 8254's channel-2 output through port
     0x61, which MACHINE.serial-timer@1 does not have (it reads 255; findings
     0008).  The wait runs out and the rate is 0, for the full ten seconds.

4. **"The door said 0 throughout" said the same thing about a machine that died
   and one that ran and measured nothing.**  The 1-second ones printed nothing.
   A builder told the door said 0 looks for another way to read the clock, and
   the builders did: their reads after those failures were of CPUID leaves,
   KVM's paravirtual clock and tsc_msr.c (the builders' logs under
   out/agent/race-timer/jobs/).

5. **The fix (decision 0112).**  TST.timer.rate@1's check now says "the machine
   printed nothing in its 10 seconds: it died or hung before the program's first
   line" for an empty capture, and "the machine stopped printing after N of its
   10 seconds" when its lines span less than half of them; only ten seconds of
   zeros is "the door said 0".  TST.timer.says@1 holds the check to that on three
   captures it makes (red with the old check; two mutations).  Checked in a copy
   in Claude's container, under emulation, not on the jig: the hand lift passes
   with the new check (drift 0 ms per 10 s), and 20261031T205725Z-timer-1's file
   is said to have printed nothing.

6. **Read by Claude: what is not fixed.**  The builder still divides 64-bit
   numbers its own way: AiOs has a divide helper, PART.divide64@1, which the
   stopwatch flavor does not link, and the lifter is not told it exists.  The
   director asks what the tests check because it is shown only notes.
   TST.timer.kvm@1's check has the same "said 0" line and is not changed here.
