# Findings 0017: the referee checked against its own rule book

Source: part 15, 2026-10-14, Chris's question "how does the ref know?"
Goes to: humans who own the constitution.

1. **What was found.**  schema/petition.md has had twelve rules since petition
   0000.  Before tonight jig/aios enforced three of them: rule 1 (every
   catalogue number resolves), rule 9 (not expired) and rule 12 (no hardware,
   part 15).  Rules 2 to 8 were written and never checked.  They were followed
   by hand, which is luck, not a guard.

2. **What was built.**  `jig/aios rules` checks rules 2 to 8 on every petition
   and writes tests/referee-results.txt, one line per rule per petition: PASS,
   or FAIL with the rule and the catalogue number.  SPEC.referee.2-8@1 says so;
   TST.referee.2-8@1 breaks petition 0001 seven ways and expects each rule
   named; seven mutations switch one rule off each and must be caught.  A post
   quotes that file, not Claude.

3. **What the file says**, at commit a2a8490, 56 lines, 9 FAIL:
   - Rules 2, 3, 5 and 7 hold in all eight petitions.  Every one of the 36
     capability requests names a spec clause that exists and belongs to a part
     in that petition.  The tenet "every capability is codified" held the whole
     way; the referee simply never said so.
   - Rules 4 and 6 fail in petitions 0004 to 0007.  PART.timer.kvmclock@1 is
     depended on by every loader from @4 and requires CAP.hv.kvmclock@1, and no
     petition lists the part or asks for the capability; 0005 also misses
     CAP.dev.pit@1 for PART.timer.tsc-pit@1.  The lists stopped growing when the
     loader started pulling in more parts than the petition named.  No code is
     affected.
   - Rule 8 fails in 0000 and 0004: SNS.hello.boot-to-output@1 and
     SNS.stopwatch.drift@1 are a code and nothing else.  Sensors were never
     built (plan 4.4).

4. **Why the four petitions were not fixed tonight.**  Making rules 4 and 6
   pass is not a list edit.  Once 0004 to 0007 ask for CAP.hv.kvmclock@1, rule 5
   asks what exposes it, and nothing in the catalogue does: no machine, no
   tool.  Under KVM the hypervisor provides it, so TOOL.qemu@1 or the machine
   entries would have to claim it, and that is a change to what the catalogue
   says the machine grants.  Writing that in so the rules pass is the loosened
   test in another shape.  It needs a decision: who exposes CAP.hv.kvmclock@1,
   and whether a machine whose spec says "no timer" exposes CAP.dev.pit@1
   because microvm forces the 8254 present.  Until then the four petitions
   stand refused by rules 4 and 6, in the referee's own file, and the build
   gate (`jig/aios check`) still enforces 1, 9 and 12 only.  Plan 3.2.

5. **Rule 13, built.**  Chris's rule, decided by voice the same night: a
   committee seat or the mechanic speaks to the referee only in a numbered
   list, one verb-noun line each.  constitution/verbs.toml holds the eight
   verbs; `jig/aios lines` refuses prose, an unknown verb, an unknown noun,
   words after the noun, a skipped number, and a verb asked for that already
   exists.  SPEC.referee.13@1, TST.referee.verb-noun@1, three mutations.  Two
   things learned building it: "never use a verb you asked for in the same
   list" needs no check of its own, because a used verb must already be in the
   constitution and one that is cannot be asked for; and the words-after-the-
   noun check is a better message, not a separate rule, since a noun with a
   space in it never resolves.  No committee exists yet, so nothing produces
   these lists today; the check waits for the first seat.

6. **What this cannot see.**  The same limits as findings 0016, plus one:
   `jig/aios rules` runs anywhere, so its file can be written on any machine.
   The one quoted in a post is the one from the jig run, like the tester's.
   The seven mutations ran on the jig; a run on a machine without QEMU reports
   every mutation KILLED because jig/test refuses to start, which proves
   nothing.

7. **Not raised tonight, on purpose.**  Nothing.  Everything found is above.
