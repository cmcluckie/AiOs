2. **What was wrong, in the 3090's words** (its comment in space_map): "A PSE
   superpage's physical base must be 4 MiB aligned (the SDM's rule); when the
   window's physical base is not, the middle chunks are mapped page by page
   instead, as a partial chunk is."  A Linux program's window starts at
   0x08048000, not on a 4 MiB line; its middle 4 MiB was mapped with one large
   entry at an address the processor does not take, and the kernel faulted
   clearing memory there for mmap2 (findings 0057 items 2 and 3).

3. **Who found it.**  The friend was called twice (findings 0057 item 1) and
   was wrong twice about the cause.  Its second briefing quoted the three lines
   of space_map that were at fault, and said their flag was wrong; the builder
   made that change (0x87 to 0x8f, which does no harm and no good) and the test
   stayed red on hand-ins 2, 3 and 4.  Between hand-in 4 and its last call the
   builder worked the alignment out for itself and wrote the page-by-page
   mapping.  It was out of calls before it could hand in; the program handed in
   what was on its disk, and that passed.  The score the run keeps says "pass"
   for the friend: it led the builder to the lines, not to the cause.  A score
   of pass, moved, same or worse cannot say that; the findings do.
