# Findings 0035: what the timer's builders were missing

Source: part 23, the rerun of the timer at b1e1f10 (decision 0113), 2026-11-07,
run 1, stopped by Chris during its third builder; the card capped at 300 W
before it, and no card failure in it.  Every number is from the builders' logs
and files under out/agent/plan-timer/jobs/.  Goes to: the architects (Chris and
Claude).

1. **Run 1: red.**  plan-timer-1: 13 hand-ins, 3 that did not build and 10 that
   ran and read 0.  plan-timer-2: 16 hand-ins, 4 that did not build, 1 that read
   0, 4 that measured a rate and failed on the first reading (53 to 75 ms,
   expected 5 or less) and 7 that ran from 0 and counted about twice the host's
   time (19,500 to 29,000 ms against 9,718 to 9,888).  plan-timer-3: 10 hand-ins
   when it was stopped, 4 that did not build and 6 that read 0.  Every test line
   they were given said what the machine did (decision 0113).

2. **Every lifter went looking for the test.**  The lifter is told what a test
   printed, never the test.  plan-timer-3 alone asked Linux's reader for list .,
   list ../, find tsccal, find stopwatch and find timer.rate, each to learn what
   it was judged by.  The director asked strategic the same on the first rerun
   (findings 0033 item 2).

3. **Each lifter started from nothing.**  plan-timer-3 was given a hint and no
   file, and its first six hand-ins went where plan-timer-2's first five had: did
   not build, read 0.

4. **The director stated a hardware fact it could not have read.**  Its hint to
   plan-timer-2 called port 0x41 "channel select".  The 8254's mode port is
   0x43 (the hand lift's PIT_MODE, tsccal.c line 96); 0x41 is its channel 1, by
   Claude's knowledge, not checked in Linux here.  plan-timer-2's file did as told: it writes 0 to port
   0x41 and reads the low byte of channel 0 twice, back to back, with no latch.
   Its rate came from counts a few ticks apart.  Read by Claude: it was not one
   wrong number away from passing; its way of measuring was wrong.  Its own
   64-bit divide, written in assembly after the linker named __udivdi3, had its
   operands right.

5. **The fixes (decision 0114).**
   - The lifter's first message shows each of the item's tests: its red_when,
     its runner, its check under tests/expected/ and every file under tests/ its
     runner names.  None is the hand lift.  A check that calls another check
     (timer.rate@1's calls loader.ms@1's) does not have that one shown.
   - From the second job on, the director's job names the last lifter's run as
     start; the lifter finds that run's file already written under its name and
     is shown it with the line the tests said of it.
   - prompts/director@3.txt: on an exam item the next lifter has the last file;
     the director states no fact about the hardware or Linux that a result note
     does not show.  A prompt, not a test: it is checked by reading the next
     run's hints.
   - agent/plans/timer.toml: the timer at b1e1f10 with director@3, on the
     layered agent alone.  It is no longer a race: jig/sit is frozen and shows a
     model none of this.
   TST.agent.lift checks the first two, each red with its fix taken out (three
   new mutations, ten in all), in Claude's container, not on the jig; agent.build,
   agent.direct, agent.limit and agent.runtime still pass there.
